# Binomial Distribution

Let

$$
X_n
\sim
\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)
$$

and define

$$
q
=
1-p.
$$

The probability of obtaining exactly $k$ successes is

$$
P(X_n=k)
=
\binom{n}{k}
p^kq^{n-k}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(X_n=k)
=
\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}
p^kq^{n-k}.
$$

The binomial mean is

$$
\mu
=
np
$$

and its variance is

$$
\sigma^2
=
npq.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sigma
=
\sqrt{npq}.
$$
