# Standard Normal Distribution

<!-- pdf-page-width: wide -->

The limiting probability density is therefore

$$
\phi(z)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
e^{-z^2/2}.
$$

The Gaussian integral proved that

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
e^{-z^2/2}\,dz
=
\sqrt{2\pi}.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\phi(z)\,dz
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
e^{-z^2/2}\,dz.
$$

Thus,

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\phi(z)\,dz
=
1.
$$

Hence,

$$
\boxed{
\phi(z)
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
e^{-z^2/2}
}
$$

is a probability density function.
