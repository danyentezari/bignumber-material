# Important Concepts in Probability Theory

In this section, some properties of probability theory are highlighted because they are relevant throughout the statistics and probability theory.

<!--
Kolmogorov axioms. Probability is a nonnegative countably additive set function assigning probability one to the sample space. This principle is used to provide the mathematical foundation from which the fundamental rules of probability follow.

Events and sample spaces. A sample space contains all possible outcomes, and events are measurable subsets of that space. This principle is used to represent probabilistic statements as sets to which probabilities can be assigned.

Independence. Events are independent when the probability of their intersection equals the product of their probabilities. This principle is used to formalize the absence of probabilistic dependence between events and random variables.

Conditional probability. Conditional probability restricts probability to outcomes compatible with a specified event of positive probability. This principle is used to describe how probabilities change when additional information is given.

Random variables. A random variable is a measurable real-valued function defined on a probability space. This principle is used to represent random outcomes numerically and induce probability distributions on the real numbers.

Expectation. Expectation assigns an integrable random variable its probability-weighted mean. This principle is used to obtain averages, moments, conditional expectations, and numerical summaries of probability distributions.

Modes of convergence. Random variables can converge almost surely, in probability, in distribution, and in mean. These modes impose different strengths of asymptotic agreement. This principle is used to formulate limiting results for sequences of random variables.

## Problems

- Obtain $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$.
- Obtain $P(A\mid B)=P(A\cap B)/P(B)$ when $P(B)>0$.
- Obtain $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$ when $A$ and $B$ are independent.
- Obtain $P(A)=\sum_i P(A\mid B_i)P(B_i)$ for a countable partition $\{B_i\}$.
- Obtain $E[X+Y]=E[X]+E[Y]$ for integrable random variables without requiring independence.
- Obtain the distribution of a random variable from $P_X(B)=P(X\in B)$.
- Establish the implications $X_n \xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}} X \implies X_n \xrightarrow{P} X \implies X_n \xrightarrow{d} X$.
