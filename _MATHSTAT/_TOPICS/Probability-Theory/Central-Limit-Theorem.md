# Central Limit Theorem

The *central limit theorem* states that when samples are drawn from a population, where the size of the samples are 30 or greater, the samples will have a normal distribution. This theorem holds even if the population from which the samples are drawn does is not normally distributed.

Central limit theorem. The standardized sum of independent identically distributed random variables with finite mean and positive finite variance converges in distribution to the standard normal distribution. This principle is used to approximate distributions of large sums by the normal distribution.

Standardized sample mean. The sample mean becomes asymptotically standard normal after subtracting the population mean and scaling by its standard error. This principle is used to approximate probabilities involving sample means and construct large-sample statistical procedures.

Convergence in distribution. Central limit theorem convergence means that the distribution functions of standardized sums converge to the standard normal distribution function at every continuity point. This principle is used to formalize the normal approximation without requiring the random variables themselves to converge pointwise.

Independence of summands. The classical central limit theorem assumes that the variables forming the sum are independent. This principle is used to control dependence between contributions so that no systematic interaction prevents the standardized sum from approaching the normal law.

Finite variance condition. The classical central limit theorem requires a finite positive variance so that fluctuations of sums have the standard square-root scaling. This principle is used to define the standardization and establish convergence to a normal distribution.

Lindeberg condition. The Lindeberg condition requires that contributions from unusually large summands become negligible relative to the total variance. This principle is used to establish a central limit theorem for independent random variables that need not have identical distributions.

Lindeberg central limit theorem. A properly standardized sum of an independent triangular array converges in distribution to the standard normal law when the total variance is normalized and the Lindeberg condition holds. This principle is used to extend normal convergence beyond identically distributed summands.
