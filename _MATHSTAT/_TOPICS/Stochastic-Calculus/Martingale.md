# Martingale

A *martingale* is, informally, a description about a sequence of random variables.

<!--
Martingale property. An integrable stochastic process is a martingale when its conditional expected future value, given all present information, equals its current value. This principle is used to model processes with no predictable gain from currently available information.

Filtration. A filtration is an increasing sequence of information sets representing everything known as time progresses. A martingale must be adapted to its filtration. This principle is used to specify precisely what information is available when conditional expectations are evaluated.

Fair-game interpretation. A martingale represents a fair game because the expected future value conditional on present information equals the present value. This principle is used to interpret martingales as processes whose past observations provide no systematic expected profit.

Martingale transformation. A predictable strategy applied to martingale increments produces another martingale under suitable integrability conditions. This principle is used to represent gains from nonanticipating strategies and construct new martingales from existing ones.

Optional stopping theorem. Under specified conditions, stopping a martingale at a random stopping time preserves its expected value. This principle is used to calculate expectations at random stopping times and analyze fair games that terminate according to predetermined rules.

Martingale convergence. Under suitable boundedness and integrability conditions, a martingale converges to a limiting random variable. This principle is used to establish limiting behavior of stochastic processes whose values evolve through successive conditional expectations.

Continuous-time martingale. A continuous-time process satisfies the martingale property when its conditional expected value at any future time equals its current value. This principle is used to study Brownian motion, stochastic integrals, and stochastic differential equations.

## Problems

- Decide whether $(X_n)$ satisfies $E(X_{n+1}\mid\mathcal F_n)=X_n$.
- Obtain $E(X_\tau)=E(X_0)$ under the conditions of the optional stopping theorem.
- Obtain the probability that a simple random walk hits $a$ before $-b$.
- Obtain almost-sure convergence of a nonnegative martingale.
- Construct a martingale from a random walk by subtracting its predictable drift.
- Identify Brownian motion as a continuous-time martingale.
