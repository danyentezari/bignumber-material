# Mass

Mass is the measure of an object's resistance to changes in its motion.

Note: Also denoted $m$.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Mass*) From Susskind and Friedman: the mass of a particle is a tag that goes with the particle and characterizes the particle itself, not the motion of the particle. It tells us that when the velocity of an object is zero, its energy is

- $E = mc^{2}$ .

where

- $m$ is the mass.
- $E$ is the energy at zero velocity.
- $c$ is the speed of light.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Mass*) From Knight: mass is an intrinsic property of an object that describes the object’s inertia and, loosely speaking, its quantity of matter. We can rewrite Newton’s second law in the form

- $F_{\mathrm{net}} = ma$ .

where

- $m$ is the mass.
- $F_{\mathrm{net}}$ is the net force.
- $a$ is the acceleration.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Mass = Inertial Mass*) From Shankar: one is the inertial mass, which is how much a body hates velocity change, how hard it resists acceleration, the mass in

- $F = ma$ .

where

- $m$ is the inertial mass.
- $F$ is the force.
- $a$ is the acceleration.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

For $F_{\mathrm{net}} = 6$ and $a = 3$,

$$
m = \dfrac{F_{\mathrm{net}}}{a} = 2
$$

where

- mass is net force divided by acceleration.

### General

For rest energy with $c = 1$ and $m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$,

$$
E = mc^{2} \in \{1, 2, 3\}
$$

where

- rest energy scales with mass.

## References
