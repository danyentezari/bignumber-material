# Momentum

Momentum is the measure of the motion of an object.

Note: Also denoted $p$. Also denoted $\mathbf{p}$. Also denoted $P$.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Momentum*) From Susskind and Friedman: momentum, often denoted by $P$, is a $3$-vector that in Newtonian physics is given by the mass times the velocity,

- $P = mv$ .

where

- $P$ is the momentum.
- $m$ is the mass.
- $v$ is the velocity.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Momentum*) From Knight: the product of an object’s mass and velocity is called the momentum of the object:

- $\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}$ .

where

- $\mathbf{p}$ is the momentum.
- $m$ is the mass.
- $\mathbf{v}$ is the velocity.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Momentum*) From Shankar: the momentum $p$ of a single particle is given by

- $p = mv$ .

where

- $p$ is the momentum.
- $m$ is the mass.
- $v$ is the velocity.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

For $m = 2$ and $v = 3$,

$$
p = mv = 6
$$

where

- momentum is mass times velocity.

### General

For masses $m \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ each with $v = 2$,

$$
p = mv \in \{2, 4, 6\}
$$

where

- each particle has momentum proportional to its mass.
