# Four Fundamental Forces

The four basic interactions of nature—the gravitational force, the electromagnetic force, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force—that is used to classify how matter and fields influence one another.

Note: Also called the four basic forces. Also called the four fundamental interactions. Overview from OpenStax; each force below is a separate concept block.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Four Basic Forces*) From OpenStax (Urone and Hinrichs): one of the most remarkable simplifications in physics is that only four distinct forces account for all known phenomena. The four basic forces are the gravitational force, the electromagnetic force, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force.

where

- these four are the basic forces of nature.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Gravitational Force*) From OpenStax (Urone and Hinrichs): the gravitational force is surprisingly weak—it is only because gravity is always attractive that we notice it at all. Our weight is the gravitational force due to the entire Earth acting on us. On the very large scale, as in astronomical systems, the gravitational force is the dominant force determining the motions of moons, planets, stars, and galaxies. The gravitational force is the only force we experience directly that is not electromagnetic.

where

- gravity is always attractive.
- weight is the gravitational force of Earth on an object.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Electromagnetic Force*) From OpenStax (Urone and Hinrichs): nearly all of the forces we experience directly are due to only one basic force, called the electromagnetic force. Electromagnetic forces can be either attractive or repulsive. Friction, tension, and all of the other classes of forces we experience directly (except gravity, of course) are due to electromagnetic interactions of atoms and molecules.

where

- electromagnetism is attractive or repulsive.
- everyday contact forces are electromagnetic.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Weak Nuclear Force*) From OpenStax (Urone and Hinrichs): the weak nuclear force is one of the four basic forces. Since the weak and strong nuclear forces act over an extremely short range, the size of a nucleus or less, we do not experience them directly, although they are crucial to the very structure of matter. These forces determine which nuclei are stable and which decay.

where

- the weak nuclear force is short-range.
- it helps determine nuclear stability and decay.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Strong Nuclear Force*) From OpenStax (Urone and Hinrichs): the strong nuclear force is one of the four basic forces. The nuclear forces are vital to the substructure of matter, but they are not directly experienced on the macroscopic scale. On a macroscopic scale, electromagnetism and gravity are the basis for all forces.

where

- the strong nuclear force is short-range.
- it is vital to the substructure of matter.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

A book on a table: weight is the gravitational force; the normal force is electromagnetic.

where

- two of the four basic forces appear.

### General

| Force | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Gravitational | weight, orbits |
| Electromagnetic | chemistry, contact |
| Weak nuclear | some decays |
| Strong nuclear | nuclear binding |

where

- nuclear forces are not felt directly at human scales.
