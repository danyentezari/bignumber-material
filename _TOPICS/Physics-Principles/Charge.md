# Charge

Charge is the measure of an object's participation in electromagnetic interactions.

Note: Also called electric charge. Also denoted $q$. Also denoted $Q$.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Charge*) From Susskind and Cabannes: to begin with, consider the electric charge. The total electric charge of a system is called $Q$. It is the standard notation for the electric charge.

where

- $Q$ is the total electric charge of a system.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Charge*) From Knight: a macroscopic object, such as a plastic rod, has charge

- $q = N_{p}e - N_{e}e = (N_{p} - N_{e})e$ ,

where $N_{p}$ and $N_{e}$ are the number of protons and electrons contained in the object.

where

- $q$ is the charge of the object.
- $N_{p}$ is the number of protons.
- $N_{e}$ is the number of electrons.
- $e$ is the elementary charge.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Charge*) From Griffiths: what is the force on a test charge $Q$ due to a single point charge $q$, that is at rest a distance away? The answer (based on experiments) is given by Coulomb’s law:

- $F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}\dfrac{qQ}{r^{2}}$ .

where

- $q$ and $Q$ are electric charges.
- $r$ is the distance between them.
- $F$ is the Coulomb force.
- $\epsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of free space.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

An object with $N_{p} = 3$ and $N_{e} = 1$ has

$$
q = (N_{p} - N_{e})e = 2e
$$

where

- $e$ is the elementary charge.

### General

Two charges $q = 2$ and $Q = 3$ separated by $r = 1$, with $\dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}} = 1$ in chosen units, give

$$
F = \dfrac{qQ}{r^{2}} = 6
$$

where

- the force scales with the product of the charges.
