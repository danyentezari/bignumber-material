# Physics Principles

A set of foundational quantities that is used to describe interactions, motion, and energy exchange in physical systems.

1\. A field assigns an influence to every point in space. This principle is used to replace instantaneous action at a distance by a local description.

2\. A potential is a number at each point from which a conservative force can be recovered. A conservative force is a force whose work around every closed path vanishes. This principle is used to compare influence from place to place.

3\. A potential difference is the change in potential between two points. This principle is used to compute the work per unit charge or mass along a path.

4\. A force is an interaction that changes the motion of a body. This principle is used to compute acceleration from Newton's second law.

Newton's second law is

$$
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}} = m\mathbf{a}
$$

where

- $\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{net}}$ is the net force.
- $m$ is the mass.
- $\mathbf{a}$ is the acceleration.

5\. The four fundamental interactions are gravity, electromagnetism, the weak nuclear force, and the strong nuclear force. This principle is used to classify every known force.

6\. Energy measures a system's capacity to produce change and is conserved in an isolated system. An isolated system exchanges neither matter nor energy with its surroundings. This principle is used to decide which processes can occur.

7\. Work is energy transferred by a force acting through a displacement. This principle is used to track energy handed from one system to another.

The work of a force is

$$
W = \displaystyle\int\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}
$$

where

- $W$ is the work.
- $\mathbf{F}$ is the force.
- $d\mathbf{r}$ is a displacement along the path.

8\. Power is the rate of energy transfer. This principle is used to compare fast and slow energy changes.

9\. Momentum is mass times velocity and is conserved in an isolated system. This principle is used to analyze collisions.

The linear momentum is

$$
\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}
$$

where

- $\mathbf{p}$ is the momentum.
- $m$ is the mass.
- $\mathbf{v}$ is the velocity.

10\. Mass measures resistance to acceleration. Charge measures participation in electromagnetic interactions. A system is the chosen collection under study. These principles are used to name the object, its inertia, and its electromagnetic coupling.

Note: These principles are the field, the potential, potential difference, force, the four fundamental interactions, energy, work, power, momentum, and the triad of mass, charge, and system.
