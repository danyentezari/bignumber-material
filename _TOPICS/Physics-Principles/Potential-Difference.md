# Potential Difference

A potential difference is the measure of the difference in influence between two points in space.

Note: Also called voltage when the influence is electric.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Potential Difference*) From Susskind and Friedman: the potential energy function is denoted by $V(x)$. In classical mechanics it is related to the force on a particle by the equation

- $F(x) = -\dfrac{\partial V}{\partial x}$ .

The values of $V$ at two points therefore differ by the change of that potential between those points.

where

- $V(x)$ is the potential energy function.
- $F(x)$ is the force.
- $x$ is the position.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Potential Difference*) From Griffiths: the potential difference between points $a$ and $b$ is equal to the work it takes, per unit charge, to carry a particle from $a$ to $b$:

- $V(b) - V(a) = \dfrac{W}{Q}$ .

Equivalently,

- $V(b) - V(a) = -\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ .

where

- $V(a)$ and $V(b)$ are the potentials at points $a$ and $b$.
- $W$ is the work done to move charge $Q$ from $a$ to $b$.
- $\mathbf{E}$ is the electric field.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Potential Difference = Voltage*) From Knight: it is customary to say that the particle moves through a potential difference $\Delta V = V_{f} - V_{i}$. The potential difference between two points is often called the voltage.

where

- $\Delta V$ is the potential difference.
- $V_{i}$ and $V_{f}$ are the initial and final potentials.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

Moving charge $Q = 2$ through work $W = 12$ gives

$$
\Delta V = \dfrac{W}{Q} = 6
$$

where

- $\Delta V$ is the potential difference between the endpoints.

### General

Three paths from $a$ to $b$ in a static field give the same $\Delta V$:

$$
V(b) - V(a) = -\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}
$$

where

- the line integral is path-independent for electrostatic $\mathbf{E}$.
