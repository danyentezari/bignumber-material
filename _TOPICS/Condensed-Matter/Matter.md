# Matter

Stuff made of atoms that can form solids, liquids, and gases. Condensed matter focuses on the condensed phases, especially solids and liquids, where many constituents interact strongly through electromagnetic forces.

Note: Condensed phases are the familiar solid and liquid forms of matter.

In a solid, atoms sit in a lasting crystalline array with long-range order. In a liquid, atoms attract but stay disordered and free to rearrange. In a gas, molecules are widely separated and move independently. Simon notes no fundamental liquid–gas split beyond density and compressibility.

Positional order of the atoms. A solid has long-range crystalline order. A liquid is dense but disordered, with only local, temporary arrangement. A gas has no lasting positional order: the molecules are separated and free.

The radial distribution function $g(r)$ is used to read that order from the density of neighbors at distance $r$. In a crystal it is sharp spikes at lattice spacings. In a liquid it shows decaying short-range peaks and tends to $1$ at large $r$. In a dilute gas it is flat near $1$ beyond the collision diameter. See [Radial Distribution Function](radial-distribution-function.html).
