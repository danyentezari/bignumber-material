# Topological Manifold

A topological manifold is a Hausdorff space that is locally homeomorphic to Euclidean space of fixed dimension that is used to introduce local coordinates.

<i>

**Definition** (_Topological Manifold_) A topological manifold is a topological space, $M$, that satisfies the following conditions:

- Locally Euclidean of Dimension $n$
- Hausdorff Space
- Second Countable

</i>

## Elementary Example
### Simple

A topological manifold is locally Euclidean, Hausdorff, and second countable. A line locally looks like $\mathbb{R}^{1}$.

$$
M = \mathbb{R}
$$

$$
n = 1
$$

$$
\text{each } p \in M \text{ has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open interval in }\mathbb{R}
$$

where

- $M$ is the manifold.
- $n$ is the dimension.

### General

The plane is a $2$-manifold: each point has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open disk in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$.

$$
M = \mathbb{R}^{2},\quad n = 2
$$

$$
U = \{ (x,y) : x^{2} + y^{2} < 1 \}
$$

where

- $U$ is an open neighborhood of the origin.
- $U$ is homeomorphic to an open set in $\mathbb{R}^{2}$.
