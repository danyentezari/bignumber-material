# Inertial Reference Frames

A coordinate system in which a free particle with no external forces stays at rest or moves in a straight line at constant speed.

1\. An inertial reference frame is a frame in which Newton’s first law holds. Newton’s first law is the statement that a body with no net force keeps constant velocity. This principle is used to test whether a given coordinate system is inertial.

2\. A frame that moves at constant velocity relative to an inertial frame is itself inertial. Constant velocity is a speed and direction that do not change with time. This principle is used to generate the family of frames in which the laws of mechanics keep the same form.

3\. In an inertial frame every acceleration comes from a real force. A real force is a push or pull from an identifiable contact or field. This principle is used to apply Newton’s second law with no extra correction terms.

Note: These principles are the law of inertia as a frame test, closure under constant-velocity boosts, and the absence of fictitious forces. Also called an inertial frame. See [Inertial Frame](Inertial-Frame.md).
