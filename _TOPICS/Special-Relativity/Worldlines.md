# Worldlines

A curve through spacetime that is used to represent the history of a particle as a one-dimensional set of events.

Note: Also called a world line. Also called the trajectory of a particle on a Minkowski diagram.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Worldline*) From Carroll: the path of a particle is a curve through spacetime, a parameterized one-dimensional set of events, called the worldline.

where

- the curve is parameterized by a real parameter.
- each point on the curve is an event.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*World Line*) From Emam: the path of a particle in spacetime diagrams is called its world line.

where

- the path is drawn in a spacetime diagram with time and space axes.
- different slopes correspond to different speeds.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*World Line*) From Griffiths: the trajectory of a particle on a Minkowski diagram is called its world line.

where

- a Minkowski diagram is a spacetime plot of events.
- the trajectory is the particle’s history on that plot.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*World Line*) From Frankel: the path $x = x(t)$ of a mass point in $M_{0}^{4}$ is called its world line.

where

- $x(t)$ is the path of the mass point.
- $t$ is the time parameter.
- $M_{0}^{4}$ is Minkowski spacetime.

</i>

## References

1. Carroll, S. M. *Spacetime and Geometry*. Cambridge University Press. — worldline as a parameterized one-dimensional set of events.
2. Emam, M. H. *Covariant Physics*. Oxford University Press, 2021. — world line as the path of a particle in a spacetime diagram.
3. Griffiths, D. J. *Introduction to Electrodynamics*. Cambridge University Press, 2024. — world line as trajectory on a Minkowski diagram.
4. Frankel, T. *The Geometry of Physics*. Cambridge University Press, 2012. — world line $x=x(t)$ of a mass point.
