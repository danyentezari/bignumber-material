# Light Cone

A cone in spacetime that is used to mark the set of events reachable from a given event by light signals.

Note: Also called the null cone. Also called the forward and backward light cones when future and past parts are distinguished.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Light Cone*) From Emam: a lightlike particle traveling at exactly the speed of light $v = c$ will have a slope of $1$. The world lines of all possible lightlike particles form a cone with unit slope in the spacetime diagram. This is called the light cone.

where

- $v$ is the particle speed.
- $c$ is the speed of light.
- the spacetime diagram uses suppressed spatial axes.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Light Cone*) From Carroll: a set of points that are all connected to a single event by straight lines moving at the speed of light is the light cone, since if we imagine including one more spatial coordinate, the two diagonal lines get completed into a cone.

where

- the single event is the apex of the cone.
- the connecting lines are lightlike.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Forward and Backward Light Cone*) From Griffiths: if we include a $y$-axis coming out of the page, the “wedges” become cones—and, with an undrawable $z$-axis, hyper-cones. Because their boundaries are the trajectories of light rays, we call them the forward light cone and the backward light cone.

where

- the forward light cone is the future lightlike boundary.
- the backward light cone is the past lightlike boundary.

</i>

## References

1. Emam, M. H. *Covariant Physics*. Oxford University Press, 2021. — lightlike world lines form the light cone of unit slope.
2. Carroll, S. M. *Spacetime and Geometry*. Cambridge University Press. — light cone as points linked to one event by lightlike lines.
3. Griffiths, D. J. *Introduction to Electrodynamics*. Cambridge University Press, 2024. — forward and backward light cones.
