# Total Energy

A relativistic energy equal to the Lorentz factor times rest energy that is used to express the full energy of a free particle including rest energy and kinetic energy.

Note: Also called relativistic energy.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Total Energy*) From Knight: let’s define the total energy $E$ of a particle to be

- $E = \gamma mc^{2} = E_{0} + K = \text{rest energy} + \text{kinetic energy}$ .

where

- $\gamma$ is the Lorentz factor of the particle.
- $m$ is the rest mass.
- $c$ is the speed of light.
- $E_{0} = mc^{2}$ is the rest energy.
- $K$ is the kinetic energy.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Relativistic Energy*) From Griffiths: Einstein identified $p^{0}c$ as relativistic energy:

- $E = \dfrac{mc^{2}}{\sqrt{1 - \dfrac{u^{2}}{c^{2}}}}$ .

where

- $m$ is the rest mass.
- $u$ is the particle speed.
- $c$ is the speed of light.
- $p^{0}$ is the time component of the energy-momentum $4$-vector.

</i>

## References

1. Knight, R. D. *Physics for Scientists and Engineers: A Strategic Approach with Modern Physics*. Pearson, 2023. — $E=\gamma mc^{2}=E_{0}+K$.
2. Griffiths, D. J. *Introduction to Electrodynamics*. Cambridge University Press, 2024. — $E=mc^{2}/\sqrt{1-u^{2}/c^{2}}$.
