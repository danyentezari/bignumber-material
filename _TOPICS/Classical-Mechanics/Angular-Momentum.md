# Angular Momentum

A vector measure of rotational motion that is used to describe how a particle’s momentum is aimed about a chosen origin.

Note: Also denoted $\mathbf{L}$. For one particle it is the cross product of position and momentum.

1. For one particle is $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$.
2. Depends on the choice of origin through the position vector $\mathbf{r}$.
3. Is perpendicular to the plane formed by $\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{p}$ when those vectors are not parallel.
4. Changes in time at a rate equal to the torque about the same origin.
5. Is conserved for a system when the total external torque vanishes.
6. Adds over particles to give the total angular momentum of a system.

## References

1. Feynman, R. P., Leighton, R. B., & Sands, M. *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, Vol. I. — for one particle, $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}$; $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \dfrac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt}$; conservation when external torque is zero.
