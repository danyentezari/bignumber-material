# Quantum Chemistry

A branch of physical chemistry that is used to determine the structures, bonding, stabilities, and light-related properties of molecules from the laws of physics.

1. Every molecule is made of positively charged nuclei and negatively charged electrons held by electrostatic forces.
2. These particles are too small for ordinary Newtonian mechanics; quantum mechanics sets their allowed energies.
3. Nuclei are far heavier than electrons, so nuclei move slowly while electrons move quickly around them.
4. Mapping where electrons are likely to be shows which atom arrangements sit lowest in energy and form stable bonds.
5. These calculations predict shapes, stabilities, and how molecules take up or give off light without always needing a lab experiment.

Note: Holding nuclei fixed while solving for electrons is the Born-Oppenheimer approximation in the sources.
