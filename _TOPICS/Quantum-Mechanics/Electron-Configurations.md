# Electron Configurations

A representation of how electrons are distributed among the allowed shells, subshells, and quantum states of an atom that is used to write the ground-state occupation of an atom.

1\. A configuration lists the occupied subshells with their electron counts, as in $1s^{2}2s^{2}2p^{6}$. A subshell is the set of orbitals sharing $n$ and $\ell$. This principle is used to summarize the Pauli filling of an atom.

2\. Each subshell holds at most $2(2\ell+1)$ electrons. This principle is used to close the $s$, $p$, $d$, and $f$ blocks.

The subshell capacity is

$$
N_{\ell} = 2(2\ell+1)
$$

where

- $N_{\ell}$ is the maximum number of electrons in the subshell.
- $\ell$ is the orbital quantum number.

3\. The configuration is built by the Aufbau order and by Hund's rule. This principle is used to write the ground configuration before spectroscopic terms.

Note: These principles are the configuration notation, subshell capacity, and construction by Aufbau and Hund's rule.
