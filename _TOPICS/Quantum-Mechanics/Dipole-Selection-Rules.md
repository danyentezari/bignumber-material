# Dipole Selection Rules

Quantum-mechanical constraints that are used to decide which single-photon electric-dipole transitions are allowed, where an electric-dipole transition is a jump driven by the operator $e\mathbf{r}$.

1\. The electric-dipole matrix element vanishes unless the orbital quantum number changes by one unit. This principle is used to forbid $\Delta\ell=0$ lines in the electric-dipole approximation.

The orbital selection rule is

$$
\Delta\ell = \pm 1
$$

where

- $\ell$ is the orbital quantum number.

2\. The magnetic quantum number can change by $0$ or $\pm 1$. This principle is used to assign polarizations to the components of a line.

The magnetic selection rule is

$$
\Delta m = 0,\pm 1
$$

where

- $m$ is the magnetic quantum number.

3\. The parity of the wavefunction must change. Parity is the even or odd character of a wavefunction under inversion. This principle is used to forbid transitions between two even states or two odd states.

Note: These principles are $\Delta\ell=\pm 1$, $\Delta m=0,\pm 1$, and the parity-change rule for electric-dipole radiation.
