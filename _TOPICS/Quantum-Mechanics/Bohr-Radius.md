# Bohr Radius

A physical constant that is used to set the length scale of the hydrogen atom, where a length scale is the typical size that appears in the ground-state wavefunction.

1\. The Bohr radius is the most probable distance of the electron from the proton in the hydrogen ground state. This principle is used to quote the size of the hydrogen atom.

The Bohr radius is

$$
a_{0} = \dfrac{4\pi\epsilon_{0}\hbar^{2}}{m_{e}e^{2}}
$$

where

- $a_{0}$ is the Bohr radius.
- $\epsilon_{0}$ is the permittivity of free space.
- $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant.
- $m_{e}$ is the electron mass.
- $e$ is the elementary charge.

2\. The numerical value is about $0.529\,\mathrm{\AA}$. This principle is used to convert atomic calculations into laboratory lengths.

3\. The hydrogen radial wavefunctions are written in units of $a_{0}$. This principle is used to scale all hydrogen lengths by one constant.

The ground-state radial scale is

$$
\psi_{100}(r) \propto e^{-r/a_{0}}
$$

where

- $\psi_{100}$ is the ground-state wavefunction.
- $r$ is the electron-proton distance.
- $a_{0}$ is the Bohr radius.

Note: These principles are the definition of the Bohr radius, its numerical size, and its role as the hydrogen length unit.
