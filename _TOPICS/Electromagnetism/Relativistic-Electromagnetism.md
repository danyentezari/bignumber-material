# Relativistic Electromagnetism

The formulation of electromagnetism in spacetime that is used to treat electric and magnetic fields as parts of one Lorentz-covariant field.

Note: Also called covariant electrodynamics. Closely related to the field tensor and four-potential.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Relativistic Electromagnetism*) From Griffiths: electricity and magnetism are unified by special relativity; $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ mix under Lorentz transformations and together form the field tensor $F^{\mu\nu}$.

where

- $F^{\mu\nu}$ packages $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Relativistic Electromagnetism*) From Emam: the electromagnetic field is described by the field strength built from the potential $4$-vector, and Maxwell's equations take a covariant form in terms of $F^{\mu\nu}$ and the current $4$-vector.

where

- $A^{\mu}$ is the four-potential.
- $J^{\mu}$ is the four-current.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Relativistic Electromagnetism*) From Susskind and Friedman: classical electromagnetism is a relativistic field theory whose fundamental object is the potential, with gauge invariance and the action principle underlying Maxwell's equations.

where

- Lorentz invariance is built into the covariant formulation.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

A pure electric field in one frame can yield both $\mathbf{E}'$ and $\mathbf{B}'$ in a boosted frame.

where

- electric and magnetic fields mix under boosts.

### General

Maxwell's equations are written covariantly as equations for $F^{\mu\nu}$ and $J^{\mu}$.

where

- the same physics holds in every inertial frame.
