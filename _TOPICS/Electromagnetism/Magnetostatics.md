# Magnetostatics

The branch of electromagnetism that studies steady currents and the magnetic fields they produce that is used when charges move at constant rates without time-varying electric fields.

Note: Also called steady-state magnetism.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Magnetostatics*) From Griffiths: magnetostatics is the study of magnetic fields produced by steady currents, governed by

- $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ ,
- $\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_{0}\mathbf{J}$ .

where

- $\mathbf{B}$ is the magnetic field.
- $\mathbf{J}$ is the steady current density.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Magnetostatics*) From Knight: when currents are steady, magnetic fields are constant in time and can be found from the Biot–Savart law or Ampère's law.

where

- $\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t} = 0$ in the magnetostatic Ampère law.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Magnetostatics*) From Susskind and Friedman: steady currents are sources of magnetic fields, and magnetostatics is the sector of Maxwell theory with time-independent $\mathbf{B}$ sourced by $\mathbf{J}$.

where

- charge density may be static while current is steady.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

A long straight wire with current $I$ produces

$$
B = \dfrac{\mu_{0}I}{2\pi r}
$$

where

- $r$ is the perpendicular distance from the wire.

### General

Steady $\mathbf{J}$ determines $\mathbf{B}$ via

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf{B} = \mu_{0}\mathbf{J},\quad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0
$$

where

- these are the magnetostatic Maxwell equations.
