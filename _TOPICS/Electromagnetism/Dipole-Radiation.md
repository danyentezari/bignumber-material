# Dipole Radiation

Electromagnetic radiation from an oscillating electric dipole that is used as the leading radiating multipole for many small sources.

Note: Also called electric dipole radiation.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Dipole Radiation*) From Griffiths: an oscillating electric dipole $\mathbf{p}(t)$ produces radiation fields that fall as $1/r$ in the radiation zone, with power proportional to the square of the acceleration of the dipole moment.

where

- $\mathbf{p}$ is the electric dipole moment.
- $r$ is the distance from the source.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Dipole Radiation*) From Knight: accelerating charges radiate, and the simplest model of a radiator is an oscillating dipole that emits electromagnetic waves at its oscillation frequency.

where

- the radiated power increases with acceleration.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Larmor Formula*) From Griffiths: a nonrelativistic accelerating charge radiates power

- $P = \dfrac{\mu_{0}q^{2}a^{2}}{6\pi c}$ ,

which underlies dipole radiation estimates.

where

- $a$ is the acceleration.
- $q$ is the charge.
- $c$ is the speed of light.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

A dipole oscillating as $p(t) = p_{0}\cos(\omega t)$ radiates at frequency $\omega$.

where

- $\omega$ is the angular frequency.

### General

In the radiation zone, $|\mathbf{E}|$ and $|\mathbf{B}|$ scale as $1/r$, so intensity scales as $1/r^{2}$.

where

- energy flux falls with distance squared.
