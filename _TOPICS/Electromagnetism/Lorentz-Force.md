# Lorentz Force

A force on a charged particle in electric and magnetic fields that is used to determine the particle's acceleration under electromagnetic influence.

Note: Also called the Lorentz force law. Also denoted $\mathbf{F}=q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})$.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Lorentz Force*) From Griffiths: the force on a charge $q$ in electric and magnetic fields is

- $\mathbf{F} = q\bigl(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\bigr)$ .

where

- $\mathbf{F}$ is the Lorentz force.
- $q$ is the charge.
- $\mathbf{v}$ is the velocity.
- $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are the fields at the particle.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Lorentz Force*) From Knight: a charged particle in electric and magnetic fields experiences the Lorentz force

- $\mathbf{F} = q\bigl(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\bigr)$ .

where

- the electric part is $q\mathbf{E}$ and the magnetic part is $q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Lorentz Force*) From Susskind and Friedman: the electromagnetic force on a charge depends on the electric field and on the velocity crossed with the magnetic field, combining into the Lorentz force law.

where

- magnetic forces do no work because they are perpendicular to $\mathbf{v}$.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

With $\mathbf{B} = \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{E} = E\hat{\mathbf{x}}$,

$$
\mathbf{F} = qE\hat{\mathbf{x}}
$$

where

- only the electric field acts.

### General

With both fields nonzero,

$$
\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{E} + q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}
$$

where

- the magnetic term depends on velocity.
