# Vector Potential

A vector field whose curl is the magnetic field that is used, with the scalar potential, to express electromagnetic fields.

Note: Also denoted $\mathbf{A}$. Also called the magnetic vector potential.

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Vector Potential*) From Griffiths: because $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$, one may write

- $\mathbf{B} = \nabla\times\mathbf{A}$

for a vector potential $\mathbf{A}$.

where

- $\mathbf{B}$ is the magnetic field.
- $\mathbf{A}$ is the vector potential.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Vector Potential*) From Susskind and Friedman: the fundamental quantity defining an electromagnetic field is the vector potential. Electric and magnetic fields are obtained from the potentials.

where

- $\mathbf{A}$ is primary in the action and gauge formulation.

</i>

<i>

**definition [d]** (*Vector Potential*) From Emam: together with the scalar potential $V$, the vector potential forms the potential $4$-vector

- $A^{\alpha} = \left(\dfrac{V}{c},\, \mathbf{A}\right)$ .

where

- $\mathbf{A}$ is the spatial part of the four-potential.

</i>

## Elementary Example

### Simple

For uniform $\mathbf{B} = B\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, one choice is

$$
\mathbf{A} = \dfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{r}
$$

where

- $\nabla\times\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{B}$.

### General

In magnetostatics with the Coulomb gauge $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = 0$,

$$
\nabla^{2}\mathbf{A} = -\mu_{0}\mathbf{J}
$$

where

- $\mathbf{J}$ is the steady current density.
